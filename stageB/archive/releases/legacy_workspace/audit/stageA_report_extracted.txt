 CookRisk Stage A
 Literature-Guided Model Benchmarking and Improvement Assessment
Executive Summary
Stage A tested whether literature-guided alternatives could improve the CookRisk molecular retention model without
changing the reviewed dataset, target definition, Phase 2 feature recipe, molecular GroupKFold validation, or treatment of
rare high-retention observations. The result is scientifically useful but negative: no challenger improved MAE, RMSE, and
R2 together. The recommended decision is RETAIN EXTRA TREES as the current selected benchmark among evaluated
configurations.
Data and Validation Lock
The experiment used 1,124 observations from 197 Canonical_Molecular_ID groups. All models used 5-fold GroupKFold
by molecular identity, log1p(Retention_Final), inverse-transformed predictions on the original retention scale, and
preprocessing fit only on each training fold. Automated QA found zero train-validation group overlaps and no duplicate OOF
record-model predictions.
Literature Evidence
The repository's Week 1 PDF documents six reviewed cooking-retention papers, rather than the requested eight; this
discrepancy is recorded, not filled with invented citations. Collectively, those papers support a multi-factor retention process
involving food, compound identity, cooking method and conditions, and caution that apparent increases may reflect
extractability, transformation or concentration changes. XGBoost was additionally justified from Chen and Guestrin (2016);
CatBoost and LightGBM were considered but not tested because compatible packages were not available in the isolated
Stage A environment.

ensembles were introduced.

How to Read the Results
Tuned XGBoost achieved the lowest OOF RMSE and highest OOF R2, but its MAE was 2.648 higher than Extra Trees (an
8.28% worsening). Random Forest and RBF-SVR did not improve the reference on the three core metrics. Selecting
XGBoost only from R2 or RMSE would obscure the worse typical absolute prediction error; therefore, it is not a
replacement.
Targeted Tuning
Only XGBoost was tuned. A compact nested 3-fold GroupKFold search was performed inside each outer training partition,
over 250/450 estimators, depth 2/3, and minimum child weight 3/8. The modal selected configuration used 450 estimators,
depth 3, min_child_weight 3, learning_rate 0.03, subsample 0.8, colsample_bytree 0.8, reg_lambda 5. This procedure did
not tune against final OOF predictions.
Robustness Findings
For tuned XGBoost, seed 42 versus 123 yielded MAE 34.612 versus 34.901, RMSE 138.695 versus 139.133, and R2 0.283
versus 0.279. The ranking is not driven by a random seed, but the model remains MAE-inferior. In a secondary top-1%
target-exclusion sensitivity check, its MAE/RMSE/R2 became 25.161/46.020/0.364, confirming rare high-retention records
strongly shape tail-sensitive metrics. These observations remained in all primary analyses.
Subgroups and Limitations
Subgroup error varied substantially: tuned XGBoost recorded MAE 73.349 for frying (n=76) and 31.255 for boiling (n=319);
compound-class MAE ranged from 7.744 for amino acids (n=68) to 55.975 for flavonoids (n=111). Limitations include only
197 molecular groups, heterogeneous literature-derived measurements, sparse process variables, and a supplied six-paper
rather than eight-paper literature review.

Final Decision
RETAIN EXTRA TREES. The XGBoost trade-off is potentially interesting for tail-focused future work, but it does not provide
a meaningful overall replacement because it increases MAE while improving only RMSE/R2. Extra Trees remains the
appropriate current selected benchmark among the tested configurations.

Important Reporting Note
The historical Phase 2 selected benchmark uses mean fold metrics, whereas Stage A's primary table uses aggregate OOF
metrics. They are both valid summaries but must not be numerically compared as though they share the same aggregation.
The Stage A report keeps this distinction explicit.
Handoff for the Food-Drug Interaction Database
The retention-prediction component is stable enough to serve as an upstream research-stage input, with uncertainty notes:
retain molecular GroupKFold validation, preserve high-retention records, use Extra Trees as the selected model, and carry
forward method/class/tail limitations. The next component should begin by defining a traceable drug, food-compound,
interaction-evidence and risk-context schema; it should not treat retention predictions as clinical interaction proof.
