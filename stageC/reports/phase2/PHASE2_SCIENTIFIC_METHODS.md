# Phase 2 Scientific Methods

## Evidence types and data flow

Phase 2 keeps four evidence types separate: (1) Phase 1B food-composition concentrations, (2) Stage B cooking-retention evidence, (3) primary-paper raw/cooked experimental observations, and (4) model predictions. The 117 observations are direct transcriptions of two primary papers, not model outputs and not Phase1B cooked values. Ingredient/prepared-beverage records are separated from ordinary foods.

## Source-pair calculation

For each primary-paper row, the source audit compares reported raw mean, cooked mean, reported SDs, replicate count, table unit, food/analyte, and cooking condition. It then independently computes:

`R_app = C_cooked_mean / C_raw_mean`

and `100 × R_app` for percent. The quotient is an **apparent concentration ratio**, not total analyte mass retention. Replicate-level covariance is not provided, so the ratio's uncertainty is not reconstructed from the marginal SDs.

### Turmeric source

Source: Zagórska et al., *Impact of Thermal Processing on the Composition of Curcuma longa Rhizome*, Table 1, Methods §§2.1–2.4 and Results §3. Two-gram fresh rhizome slices were boiled at 100 °C, fried on a dry ceramic pan without oil, or microwaved at 800 W for 1, 2, 5, or 10 min; microwave samples at 10 min charred and were omitted. Processes were repeated three times. Methanol extraction, 10-min ultrasound, double centrifugation, filtration, LC-MS identification, and HPLC quantification are reported. Table 1 reports mg/g plant material, mean ± SD, n=3. The source does not give cooked sample mass yield or explicitly state the cooked denominator as fresh weight or dry weight; the ratios therefore remain concentration ratios only.

### Pumpkin-leaf source

Source: Stryjecka et al., *Effects of Household Cooking Methods on the Nutritional, Phytochemical and Functional Properties of Cucurbita maxima Leaves*, Tables 2–3, Methods §§3.1–3.5 and §3.12. Leaves from five plants were pooled per biological replicate; three biological replicates were divided across raw, boiling, steaming, microwaving, and stir-frying treatments. Boiling: 100 g leaves/150 mL water, approximately 95 °C, 10 min. Steaming: 98 °C, 10 min. Microwave: 100 g leaves/12 mL water, 700 W, 3 min. Stir-fry: 100 g leaves/10 mL extra-virgin olive oil, approximately 150 °C, 2 min. Post-cooking samples were frozen, lyophilized, ground, and stored at −20 °C; water/condensate/oil were discarded. Amino acids are mg/g dry weight; phenolics are mg/100 g dry weight. Tables report mean ± SD, n=3 biological replicates, each assay in triplicate. The paper calls ratios apparent retention and the values are on dry weight; loss/yield into discarded cooking media is not included.

## Phase1B-to-retention compatibility

Before any `C_cooked = C_raw × R` operation, require exact analyte identity, food matrix/part, raw/cooked state, matching method/conditions, compatible denominator, and quantitative retention evidence. For interval evidence, retain interval bounds and reverse the loss interval correctly; do not imply a probability distribution. No candidate currently passes every gate. The exact combination and reason codes are in `retention_compatibility_matrix_2026-11-03.csv`; condition-specific primary-source scenarios are in `source_condition_level_retention_candidates_2026-11-03.csv`.

## Model

Model inference is a separate evidence path. The selected ExtraTrees pipeline requires a 33-column feature row generated by the recorded Stage A and Stage B feature code. It is not run until the exact training snapshot, artifact, preprocessing, and environment are hash-verified. OOF predictions are validation data only and cannot stand in for a new-record prediction.
