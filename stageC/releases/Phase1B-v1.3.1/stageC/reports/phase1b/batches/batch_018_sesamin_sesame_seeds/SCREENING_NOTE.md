# Batch 018 — sesamin in sesame seeds

## Screening decision

Kim et al. measured individual sesamin by HPLC-DAD using an authentic sesamin reference standard. Across 21 commercial-grade sesame-seed samples from eight countries, the reported mean was 3816.7 mg/kg, SD 1414.2 mg/kg, range 1568.4–7746.8 mg/kg. The existing aggregate candidate row is now mapped to Stage B Sesamin (`CHEM-PUBCHEM-72307`; exact Stage B identity audit complete).

The value remains held. The paper does not specify whether the commercial seed samples were raw, roasted, or otherwise processed, nor the fresh/dry moisture basis for the reported seed concentration. The record preserves the reported as-analyzed mg/kg basis and aggregate statistics without converting them or presenting the mean as one sample. Candidate ingestion is still gated on the Stage B checksum reconciliation.

## Source and sample scope

Kim, A.-Y., Yun, C.-I., Lee, J.-G., and Kim, Y.-J. (2020). “Determination and Daily Intake Estimation of Lignans in Sesame Seeds and Sesame Oil Products in Korea.” *Foods*, 9(4), 394. DOI: 10.3390/foods9040394; PMID: 32235516. Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7230771/.

The 21 commercial-grade seed samples were harvested 2015–2017 and originated from India (7), China (3), Korea (3), Ethiopia (2), Pakistan (2), Sudan (2), Nigeria (1), and Paraguay (1). Sesame seeds were powdered; oil was extracted from 5 g by Soxhlet with n-hexane and analyzed by HPLC-DAD. The study reports sesamin separately with an authentic standard. The one candidate row represents the source's aggregate mean, not 21 invented sample rows.
