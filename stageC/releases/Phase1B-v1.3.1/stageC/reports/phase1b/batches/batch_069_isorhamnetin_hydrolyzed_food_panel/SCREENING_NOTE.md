# Batch 069 — Isorhamnetin in edible foods (acid-hydrolysis candidates)

## Source and extraction

Cao J, Chen W, Zhang Y, Zhang Y, Zhao X. “Content of Selected Flavonoids in 100 Edible Vegetables and Fruits.” *Food Science and Technology Research* 16(5):395–402 (2010). DOI: 10.3136/fstr.16.395. [Publisher full text and PDF](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/fstr/16/5/16_5_395/_pdf). The full-text Table 2 provides 43 positive food/season-specific isorhamnetin values as mg/kg fresh weight, mean ± SD, n=3. Values were transcribed only where a numeric positive mean is given; dashes (below detection) were not turned into zeros.

The study analyzed fresh edible food samples from retail outlets in three randomly selected Harbin regions; each regional sample was a composite from three outlets. Food was freeze-dried for analysis, with fresh-weight results reported. The HPLC-PDA method used an authentic external isorhamnetin standard, retention time and diode-array spectral comparison, and detection at 365.7 nm. The reported isorhamnetin detection limit is 3.0 mg/kg FW.

## Critical interpretation hold

The extraction includes acid hydrolysis (ethanol/water/HCl, 90°C, 3 h). The authors state that after hydrolysis only aglycones were detected, so the table reflects hydrolysis-released total aglycone values. The source does not distinguish native free isorhamnetin from isorhamnetin released from glycosides. The exact standard/Stage B chemical identity is mapped, but these rows are **held** and must not be treated as native free-isorhamnetin concentrations until target scope explicitly permits total-aglycone-equivalent measurements.
