# Batch 124 — final review of the five uncovered identity-complete compounds

**Outcome: no new accepted measurements.** All five gaps received an individual decision after a focused source pass. Coverage remains **125/130** (143 total compounds; 130 identity-complete; 13 identity-pending). No food-composition records or mappings were added.

| Compound | Decision | Evidence and reason |
|---|---|---|
| Oroxylin A (`MOL-V10-5320315`) | Hold | Shen et al. (2019) provide a directly readable quantitative table: root mean 0.53 ± 0.52 mg/g; stems, leaves and flowers are reported as not detected. The paper discusses aerial material used as Huangqin tea, but gives no positive tea-use aerial-part value. The root result is medicinal material, not an ordinary-food measurement; non-detects are not positive quantitative values. |
| 7-Methoxyflavanone (`MOL-V10-2825024`) | Hold | Exact-identity searches found no calibrated quantitative concentration in an ordinary food. Related methoxyflavanones and 7-methoxyflavone are structurally distinct and cannot substitute. |
| 5,4′-Dihydroxy-3,6,7,8,3′-pentamethoxyflavone (`MOL-V12-CID-100625`) | Hold | Citrus literature supplies qualitative identification leads, but no verified authentic-standard/calibrated concentration for this exact positional isomer. A recent peel profiling source relies on qualitative/relative signals and does not establish an admissible quantitative result. |
| Dodecyl gallate (`MOL-V15-CID-14425`) | Hold | Edible-oil and bakery literature located in this pass was method validation, spike/recovery, or non-quantitative screening. A market-oil compendium mentions a suspected peak estimated below 0.5 ppm, explicitly unconfirmed and below its calibration range; this is not an accepted measurement. |
| Octyl gallate (`MOL-V15-CID-61253`) | Hold | Food studies located report method validation or standard additions/spike recovery in commercial foods, not native unspiked sample concentrations. No positive native-food sample value with verified exact analyte and quantitative method was found. |

## Sources checked

- Shen Y, et al. (2019), “Simultaneous determination of 15 flavonoids from different parts of *Scutellaria baicalensis* and its chemometrics analysis,” *Chinese Herbal Medicines* 11(1):20–27, [DOI 10.1016/j.chmed.2018.09.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chmed.2018.09.005), [full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328114605_Simultaneous_determination_of_15_flavonoids_from_different_parts_of_Scutellaria_baicalensis_and_its_chemometrics_analysis). Table 3 reports Oroxylin A at 0.53 ± 0.52 mg/g in root and undetected in stem, leaf, and flower; the paper's methods/results describe 77 batches and Huangqin Tea context.
- Kim JM, et al. (2016), edible-oil SPA method validation, *Food Chemistry* 213:19–25, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27451150/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodchem.2016.06.053). The accessible primary abstract reports validation/recovery and uncertainty, not native sample-level gallate results.
- Sin YP, et al. (2006), edible-oil method validation, *Journal of Food Composition and Analysis* 19:784–791, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfca.2005.12.005). The abstract describes analytical protocol validation.
- Rafecas M, et al. (1998), bakery-product method, *Journal of Chromatography A* 822:305–309, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9809448/). The accessible record describes method performance, not positive measured food concentrations.
- Sivasankaran U, et al. (2016), octyl gallate in fats and oils, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12161-015-0356-7). The food table uses standard additions and recovery values.
- Search-only/unconfirmed dodecyl gallate lead: [Food Fraud Edible Oils Compendium](https://pepolska.pl/app/uploads/2020/03/NOTA-ALL-Oleje-jadalne-kompendium-not-aplikacyjnych-2.pdf), which says the suspected peak was below calibration and not further verified. Excluded.
- Exact compound identity reference for 7-methoxyflavanone: [PubChem CID 2825024](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/summary/summary.cgi?cid=2825024). This is an identity resource, not food-composition evidence.
