# Batch 116 — red-clover irilone

## Decision

Accept one literature-level quantitative leaf summary for irilone, raising identity-complete coverage from 120 to 121 / 130. The value is 0.06 mg/g dry weight for *Trifolium pratense* leaves.

The primary analytical study by Tsao et al. (2006; DOI 10.1021/jf0614589) measured leaves, flowers, petioles, and stems from 13 cultivars at two growth stages by HPLC-DAD-MS, and its abstract identifies irilone among the measured minor aglycones. The accessible numerical transcription is the American Herbal Pharmacopoeia Red Clover monograph (2017), Table 3, which explicitly attributes 0.06 mg/g dry weight in leaves to Tsao et al. A 2023 primary food-context study documents red clover leaves and flowers as edible and consumed in salads, tea, or syrup (Mikulić et al., *Foods* 2024;13:103; DOI 10.3390/foods13010103).

The compound mapping uses the exact Stage B V20 identity (PubChem CID 5281779; InChIKey NUGRQNBDTZWXTP-UHFFFAOYSA-N). The record is a literature summary for edible leaf material, not a specific measured cultivar or food lot.

## Source and limitations

- Tsao R, Papadopoulos Y, Yang R, Young JC, McRae K. “Isoflavone profiles of red clovers and their distribution in different parts harvested at different growing stages.” *J Agric Food Chem.* 2006;54(16):5797–5805. DOI: 10.1021/jf0614589; PMID 16881680. The study abstract confirms the plant parts, 13 cultivars, HPLC-DAD-MS approach, and irilone as a measured aglycone.
- American Herbal Pharmacopoeia. *Red Clover* monograph (2017), Table 3: 0.06 mg/g dry weight in leaves, attributed to Tsao et al. 2006.
- Mikulić et al. “Phytochemical Composition of Different Red Clover Genotypes Based on Plant Part and Genetic Traits.” *Foods.* 2024;13(1):103. DOI 10.3390/foods13010103. The article documents leaves/flowers as edible and notes salads, tea, or syrup use.

The primary study’s cultivar-level numerical table was not directly accessible in this review, so the concentration is transparently identified as transcribed from the cited pharmacopoeial monograph. The underlying averaging rule, exact n for this summary, and geography are not established; the value is not sample-specific. Dry-weight basis is retained without fresh-weight conversion.
