# Batch 052 — Phloretin source screen (apple)

The primary 2021 study measured phloretin in four apple-tree cultivars. Phloretin was found only in leaves (maximum 2.8 ± 1.4 mg/g dry weight in cv. Jonagold) and was not detected in bark or twigs. In the whole apple fruits sampled from five trees pooled per cultivar, phloretin was not detected; the study's whole-fruit passage reports phloridzin values but no phloretin value.

The leaf values are not accepted as ordinary apple-food exposure: the source characterizes leaves as renewable plant material and proposes them for cosmetics, nutraceuticals or supplements, but does not establish ordinary human food consumption or a tea-use basis. No quantitative candidate row was prepared from these values, and ND was not interpreted as zero. This screen narrows the source support for the existing apple/pear food context but does not establish absence across all foods.

Source: Cacak-Pietrzak et al. (2021), *Molecules* 26(13):3902, DOI 10.3390/molecules26133902, PMID 34206687. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8270342/).


## Later evidence update (Batch 057)

A separate primary study subsequently reported phloretin aglycone in peel and flesh of 11 apple cultivars on a fresh-weight basis; see Batch 057. The Batch 052 finding remains accurate for the specific four-cultivar leaf/whole-fruit study screened here and must not be read as a general absence in apple fruit.
