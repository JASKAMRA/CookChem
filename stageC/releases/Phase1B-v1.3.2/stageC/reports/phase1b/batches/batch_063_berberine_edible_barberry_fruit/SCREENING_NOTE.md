# Batch 063 — Berberine in edible barberry fruit

## Candidate values
Miłek et al. (2023) report berberine in two *Berberis vulgaris* fruit samples: **5.46 mg/100 g** for wild-collected fruit that was freeze-dried before analysis and **1 mg/100 g** for commercially purchased dried fruit. These were normalized arithmetically to **54.6** and **10 mg/kg** on the same lyophilized/dried-fruit basis; no fresh-weight conversion was made. The results section gives those means, while Figure 4 identifies mean ± SD from three analytical replicates. Numeric SDs were not available in the extracted text and were not inferred.

## Method and review flags
The study extracted 5 g milled lyophilized fruit in 25 mL 70% ethanol using two ultrasound cycles. HPTLC densitometry used a 99% berberine sulfate standard and a 0.5–2 µg calibration range (R²=0.9906); the sample band was assigned using matching Rf. The paper notes an additional yellow alkaloid band. Hold both rows for confirmation that reported mass is berberine cation rather than sulfate-standard equivalent, and for ordinary-food provenance review of the dried commercial sample. No Stage B ingestion was attempted because the checksum gate remains unresolved.

The 2018 *B. vulgaris* edible-berry study reporting 0.36 mg/kg described the result for an ethanolic extract, with unclear fruit-vs-extract denominator; it was not used as a fruit concentration. The 2023 paper is preferable because it reports results by fruit sample.
