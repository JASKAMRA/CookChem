# Batch 055 — commercial culinary thyme: thymol and carvacrol

## Source

Sgorbini B, Bicchi C, Cagliero C, Cordero C, Liberto E, Rubiolo P. “Herbs and spices: characterization and quantitation of biologically-active markers for routine quality control by multiple headspace solid-phase microextraction combined with separative or non-separative analysis.” *Journal of Chromatography A*. 2015;1376:9–17. DOI: 10.1016/j.chroma.2014.12.007. Author manuscript: https://iris.unito.it/handle/2318/152493

## Screen outcome

The primary study analyzed nine commercial *Thymus vulgaris* lots supplied by Cannamela (Italy). It used authentic thymol and carvacrol standards and MHS-SPME-GC-MS directly on solid herb material. Table 2 reports separate sample-specific-Q concentrations for each marker in mg/g. Candidate file records those nine values independently for each compound and normalizes mg/g × 1,000 to mg/kg. The article distinguishes thymol from carvacrol with separative GC-MS; the non-separative method's combined signal is not used.

- Carvacrol (lots 1–9): 6.2, 5.7, 3.6, 6.3, 4.1, 0.58, 0.39, 1.2, 17.0 mg/g.
- Thymol (lots 1–9): 0.33, 0.39, 0.61, 0.54, 0.78, 3.9, 8.2, 7.5, 0.71 mg/g.

## Hold / review

The source says the lots are commercial thyme spice and directly analyzes the plant material, supporting a food-ingredient context. It does not state the samples' moisture content or explicitly say whether the retail lots were dried/as-sold, and it does not provide consumer-facing brand/culinary grade details. For that reason candidates retain an as-supplied solid-matrix label and are not accepted pending basis/scope review. The listed nine samples are commercial lots, not nine biological replicates of one cultivar. The analytical three-extraction series is not treated as biological n. No Stage C accepted coverage changes; Stage B checksum gate still blocks connector ingestion.
