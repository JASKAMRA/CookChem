# Batch 044 — Ellagic acid and isoflavones in Canadian small fruits

## Screening decision

Prepared 40 positive numeric candidate rows from Kodikara et al. (2025), Tables 2a and 2b: ellagic acid (4), daidzein (3), daidzin (14), genistein (5), and glycitein (14). Exact analyte labels, mean ± SD, and mg/kg dry-weight units are transcribed from the table. Entries reported as NF, <LOQ, or <LOD were excluded rather than encoded as zero or numerical measurements.

## Source and sample handling

Kodikara C, Sura S, et al. “Bioactive Compounds of Indigenous Canadian Small Fruits: UHPLC-HRMS-Based Phytochemical Characterization, Mineral Composition and Their Antioxidant Activity.” *ACS Food Science & Technology*. 2025;5(12):4467–4487. DOI: 10.1021/acsfoodscitech.5c00610. https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsfoodscitech.5c00610

The primary study analyzed 14 ripe edible-stage Canadian prairie fruits, collected within about 100 km of Winnipeg, Manitoba, freeze-dried, ground with seeds, and reported table concentrations on a dry-weight basis. The study identifies and quantifies phenolic compounds by UHPLC-HRMS using retention time, accurate mass, fragment ions and calibration standards. Table 2a and 2b headers state n=3.

## Review flags

Stage B registry names, PubChem CIDs and InChIKeys are attached per analyte; candidate mappings remain pending. Review analyte-specific calibration/standards, n=3 replicate independence (Methods describes one composite sample per fruit type), and the admissibility of dry-basis values. Keep daidzin distinct from daidzein and do not interpret reported glycoside as aglycone equivalent. Ellagic acid is the exact table label; free vs hydrolysis-released form should be checked against sample prep before acceptance. No fresh-weight conversion or population extrapolation was made. These candidates do not change accepted coverage; Stage B checksum gate remains in force.
