# Batch 043 — Fisetin in Canadian prairie small fruits

## Screening decision

Prepared 13 candidate food records from the exact-analyte fisetin row in Tables 2a and 2b of Kodikara et al. (2025), covering blackcurrant, wild blueberry, wild raspberry, highbush cranberry, chokecherry, gooseberry, haskap berry, nannyberry, redcurrant, Saskatoon berry, buckthorn, snowberry and wild grapes. Values are the table's mean ± SD in mg/kg dry weight. The 14th matrix (chokeberry) is reported as <LOD and is intentionally not encoded as zero or a numeric candidate.

## Source and sample handling

Kodikara C, Sura S, et al. “Bioactive Compounds of Indigenous Canadian Small Fruits: UHPLC-HRMS-Based Phytochemical Characterization, Mineral Composition and Their Antioxidant Activity.” *ACS Food Science & Technology*. 2025;5(12):4467–4487. Published online 2025-10-13. DOI: 10.1021/acsfoodscitech.5c00610. Article: https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsfoodscitech.5c00610.

The study reports 14 ripe edible-stage Canadian prairie small fruits collected within about 100 km of Winnipeg, Manitoba. Samples were freeze-dried, ground with seeds, and Table 2a/2b concentrations are explicitly normalized to dry weight. UHPLC-HRMS is the stated analytical platform; quantitative identification used retention time, accurate mass, fragment ions, and calibration standards.

## Review flags

Exact target name and PubChem CID match Stage B Fisetin (CID 5281614). Before any ingestion, verify the fisetin-specific standard/calibration detail in Table S2, resolve whether table n=3 denotes analytical or independent biological replicates (Methods describes a combined/composite sample for each fruit), and decide whether dry-basis concentrations fit Phase 1B's accepted measurement policy. No wet-basis conversion or general-population extrapolation was made. These are candidates only and do not increase accepted coverage; the Stage B checksum gate remains in force.
