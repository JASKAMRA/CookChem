# Batch 061 — allicin and diallyl sulfides in raw garlic paste

## Source

Zhang B, Zheng Z, Liu N, Liu P, Qiu Z, Qiao X. “Effect of different combined mechanical and thermal treatments on the quality characteristics of garlic paste.” *Journal of Food Science and Technology*. 2021;58:1061–1071. DOI: 10.1007/s13197-020-04620-1; PMID 33678889. Primary article: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7884543/

## Values prepared

The Results text reports raw-garlic OSC means: allicin 5.59 ± 0.26 mg/g, diallyl disulfide 0.14 ± 0.01 mg/g and diallyl trisulfide 0.22 ± 0.02 mg/g. The source reports all OSC concentrations on a dry-weight basis and says sample moisture was determined by freeze-drying. These were prepared as three held candidates, converted arithmetically to 5590, 140 and 220 mg/kg DW respectively.

## Analytical evidence and limitations

The study says individual OSCs were quantified with reference standards by HPLC-UV; allicin was synthesized from DADS, purified and identified by LC-APCI-MS/MS (92% purity), while purchased DADS and DATS standards were ≥98% purity. Raw paste was made by blending 100 g garlic cloves with 50 mL deionized water. The paste dilution and the paper’s DW denominator need explicit review before ingestion. Replicate preparations are reported as triplicate, but biological independence is unclear; allicin may transform during homogenization/extraction.

Candidates remain pending denominator/basis review and Stage B checksum-gated ingestion. They do not increase accepted coverage.
