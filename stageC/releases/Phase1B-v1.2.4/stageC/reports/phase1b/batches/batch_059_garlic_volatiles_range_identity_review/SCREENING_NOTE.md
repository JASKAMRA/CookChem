# Batch 059 — raw garlic volatile sulfides: aggregate-range and identity review

## Source

Park S, Kim H-W, Lee CJ, Kim Y, Sung J. “Profiles of volatile sulfur compounds in various vegetables consumed in Korea using HS-SPME-GC/MS technique.” *Frontiers in Nutrition*. 2024;11:1409008. DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2024.1409008. Full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1409008/full

## Screen outcome

The study analyzed raw garlic, garlic clove, garlic sprout and garlic root samples (#15–#18) supplied by Korea’s Rural Development Administration. Fresh samples were ground under liquid nitrogen, extracted by HS-SPME and analyzed by GC-MS. The chemicals section lists authentic diallyl sulfide, diallyl disulfide and diallyl trisulfide standards; quantification is described as based on standard calibration curves. The paper reports the following aggregate ranges across the four raw garlic matrices:

- Diallyl sulfide: 1.55 ± 0.01 to 2.27 ± 0.03 mg/100 g.
- Diallyl disulfide: 15.47 ± 1.18 to 21.24 ± 1.32 mg/100 g.
- “Allyl trisulfide”: 14.56 ± 0.07 to 52.67 ± 3.36 mg/100 g in the Results text; the chemical-standard and calibration sections use “diallyl trisulfide.” This naming mismatch needs confirmation before exact identity assignment.

The main text does not map each endpoint to sample #15, #16, #17 or #18. No sample-level candidate rows were created and no aggregate ranges were split into fabricated point records.

## Analytical limitation and next step

The study says it profiles overall VSC composition, uses cabbage juice for matrix validation, and recommends future isotope-labelled internal standards for more definitive absolute quantification. Quantitative ranges are useful screening evidence but should remain provisional until Supplementary Table S2 is inspected for individual sample values, the concentration denominator/basis is confirmed, and the “allyl trisulfide” label is reconciled. No accepted coverage increase.
