# Batch 014 — native Australian fruit and spice phenolic panel

Ali et al. (2023), DOI 10.3390/antiox12020254, PMID 36829816. Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9952698/ . The study analyzed mountain pepper berries (whole dried), rosella (freeze-dried powder), lemon aspen (freeze-dried powder), and strawberry gum (finely ground). Phenolics were profiled by LC-ESI-QTOF-MS/MS; sample extracts were prepared in triplicate. The authors call the individual phenolic results a quantification/semi-quantification panel and report µg/g.

Accepted 19 food records covering 11 exact Stage B analytes. Each is a food-specific numerical value stated explicitly in §3.5 and checked against the primary text; means and SDs preserved in extraction notes. Concentration is preserved as reported per gram of analyzed source sample. The study materials were dried/freeze-dried/finely ground; no conversion to fresh-weight basis or moisture adjustment was made.

The article says chlorogenic acid was lowest in strawberry gum but gives no exact value in the narrative; not ingested. Heatmap-only statements for epicatechin and stereospecific catechins/gallocatechins do not supply exact values. Glycosides were not mapped to aglycones. No serving/exposure estimate is supported by these dried ingredient samples.
