# Batch 121 — Prunin in Maazoun melon seeds: identity and source review

**Decision: held; no accepted measurement added.** Coverage remains 124/130.

## Candidate

Secondary reviews attribute 4.30 ± 0.00 mg/100 g in *Cucumis melo* (Maazoun) seeds to “naringenin-7-O-glycoside,” citing Mallek-Ayadi et al. (2018), *Chemical composition and bioactive compounds of Cucumis melo L. seeds: potential source for new trends of plant oils*, DOI [10.1016/j.psep.2017.09.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psep.2017.09.016). A review table repeats the value and reports it as seed phenolic content. The food matrix itself is plausible: melon seeds are an edible food ingredient.

## Why this does not count as Prunin

The target registry entry is Prunin (naringenin-7-O-β-D-glucoside; CID 92794). “Naringenin-7-O-glycoside” does not specify the sugar identity or linkage. It can describe more than one glycoside, including compounds with different sugars. Accessible primary-source material did not verify that the assay used authentic Prunin as its calibration standard or otherwise established the β-D-glucoside structure. The reported number is therefore not safely assignable to the exact Stage B target.

The primary article's full table and standard list could not be verified from accessible full text in this screen. Secondary sources were used only to locate the candidate, not as acceptance evidence. No record or mapping was added, and the 130-compound minimum remains unmet.

## Reconsideration condition

Reopen only if the primary full text, supplement, or author-provided analytical records establish the exact analyte as naringenin-7-O-β-D-glucoside/Prunin and confirm the reported food-basis concentration and calibration/identification method.
