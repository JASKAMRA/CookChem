# Batch 037 — β-sitosterol and stigmasterol in edible oils, nuts and seeds

## Screening decision

Prepared 35 candidate records from two primary food-composition studies:

- 14 commercial edible oils × separately quantified β-sitosterol and stigmasterol = 28 candidates. Mo et al. (2013) reports Table 1 values in µg/g oil (numerically identical to mg/kg), with means ± SD and separate analyte-specific LC-MS-MS SRM transitions.
- Seven commercial nuts/seeds with β-sitosterol only = 7 candidates. Delgado-Zamarreño et al. (2016) reports Table 3 results in mg/100 g; values were converted ×10 to mg/kg. The study's ST+CA column combines stigmasterol and campesterol due to co-elution and is excluded from stigmasterol candidates.

All records are pending Stage B identity mapping and the release checksum gate. Replicate SDs are not interpreted as between-lot variation.

## Sources and methods

Mo S, Dong L, Hurst WJ, van Breemen RB. “Quantitative analysis of phytosterols in edible oils using APCI liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry.” *Lipids*. 2013;48(9):949–956. DOI 10.1007/s11745-013-3813-3; PMID 23884629. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4073239/. Fourteen commercial oils were tested. Oils were saponified in ethanolic KOH, extracted with hexane and analyzed by APCI LC-MS-MS with individual SRM transitions for β-sitosterol and stigmasterol.

Delgado-Zamarreño MM et al. “Determination of tocopherols and sitosterols in seeds and nuts by QuEChERS-liquid chromatography.” *Food Chemistry*. 2016;192:825–830. DOI 10.1016/j.foodchem.2015.07.083; PMID 26304416. https://gredos.usal.es/bitstream/handle/10366/155686/Food%20chemistry.pdf?sequence=1. The method measures free phytosterols in natural form using QuEChERS and HPLC-DAD.

## Limitations

These studies analyze single commercial samples/products by category, not representative independent market lots. Brand/source origin is incompletely reported. Preserve reported basis and technical-replicate caveats. Stigmasterol in the nuts/seeds study is not separable from campesterol in its reported combined column and is not attributed to stigmasterol.
